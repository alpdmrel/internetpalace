A learning project rather than a product: winding a brushless motor from raw stator, magnets
and wire to see where the performance actually comes from.

It is worth doing once. Pole and slot counts, winding pattern and air gap stop being
datasheet parameters and become things you have physically made a choice about — and the
tolerance you can hold by hand on an air gap explains a great deal about why commercial motors
cost what they do.

This work turned out to be directly relevant later: my current role involves designing BLDC
motor controllers and bringing motor manufacturing in-house, and having wound one badly by hand
is useful background for specifying one properly.

## Why it is on hold

It achieved what it was for. Parked as a completed experiment rather than an unfinished build.
